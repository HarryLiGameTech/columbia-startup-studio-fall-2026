# Usability Test Script Template

Use this script for every usability session in Homework 7.

**Three rules for the person running the session:**

- Ask them to think out loud. You will have to ask many times throughout the session.
- Don't help. Their confusion is important signal. If they ask you a question, deflect back to them. "How would you think about this if I weren't here?"
- If they're stuck, ask "What are you looking for?" Then stop talking.

---

## Sample script

**Team:** PantryPal  
**Product:** A web app that suggests dinners from what's already in your fridge  
**Aha moment:** The user types in 3 or 4 ingredients and sees a dinner they could cook tonight.

### Before the session

- [ ] Consent form signed. Recording consent box checked (or not).
- [ ] Screen and audio recording started (Loom, QuickTime, or Zoom).
- [ ] Product open in a fresh browser window. Logged out.
- [ ] Note-taker ready with the [usability notes template](template_usability-notes.md).

### Brief the participant (read this out loud, verbatim)

> "Thanks for helping us today by testing our product. I want to start by saying that we are testing the product, not you. There are no wrong answers. If something is confusing, that is helpful for us to know.  
>  
> If you are uncomfortable with any part of the usability test, you do not have to continue.  
>  
> I'll ask you to please think out loud as you go. Say what you're looking at and what your thought process is. I might remind you during the testing to just say what you're thinking.  
>  
> I won't help you during the test. That might feel awkward. We want to see how the product works on its own, not how well I can explain it.  
>  
> We're recording the screen and audio so we can review it later, as the consent form says. Any questions before we start?  
>  
> Here's the list of tasks I would like for you to complete..."

### Tasks

Write out a list of tasks for them to perform, starting at your homepage, probably including account creation, and including trying to get them to your aha moment. You may only have 3-5 tasks total that can be accomplished in 20 min.

**Task 1: Sign up**  
**Say:** "Go ahead and create an account."  
**Watch for:**
- Where do they pause?
- Do they skip or question any field?
- Do they finish, or give up?

**Task 2: Get a dinner idea (aha moment)**  
**Say:** "Think about what's in your fridge and pantry. Find something you could make for dinner tonight."  
**Watch for:**
- Do they find where to enter ingredients?
- Do they reach a recipe they say they'd actually cook?
- What is the first moment they seem to get it?

### Post-session questions

Ask a few questions to get deeper on what they understood and not. You can also ask contextual questions here, about their life, when they encounter this problem, what tools they currently use to solve the problem.

1. "In your own words, what does this product do?"
2. "What was the first moment you understood what this is for?"
3. "What was the most confusing part?"
4. "Is there anything you expected to find that wasn't there?"
5. "Would you use this again? Why or why not?"
6. "If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing, what would it be?" (always ask this)

### Close

> "Thank you. This was really helpful. Any other feedback you'd like to add?"

Stop the recording. Write a short summary right away while it's fresh.
