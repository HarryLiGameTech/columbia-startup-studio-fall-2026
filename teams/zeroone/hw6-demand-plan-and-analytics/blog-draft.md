# Season-long calls: how to write a prediction your group can settle in April

On Tuesday night the Knicks raise a championship banner at the Garden, and then they start defending it. Somewhere in every group chat, someone will type a take about this season. "Easy repeat." "No chance, the East is better now." "Best record in the league, write it down."

Nobody will write it down. And in April, when the regular season ends, everyone will remember having said the right thing.

We are a student team at Columbia building CallIt, a shared record of a group's predictions. Before we wrote any code, we talked to friend groups and read how people handle friendly predictions online. The pattern surprised us. People rarely argue about the result. They argue about what everyone agreed to in the first place.

A call made in October and judged in April is the hardest case. Here is how to write one your group can actually settle.

## Why long calls fall apart

A call made at dinner gets settled by dessert. A call made on opening night has six months to drift.

By April, three things have happened. The exact wording is gone, buried thousands of messages up. The person who made the call remembers a stronger version of it, and the people who doubted remember a weaker one. And something happened that nobody planned for: an injury, a trade, a tie in the standings. Now the group has to decide what the call meant, after everyone already knows who it favors.

Screenshots help with the first problem. They do nothing for the other two.

## Four things to write down on day one

**1. The question, phrased so it has one answer.** "The Knicks will be good" is a mood, not a call. "Will the Knicks finish with the best record in the East?" can only go one way.

**2. The choices.** For a yes or no question this is easy. For "Who finishes first in the East?", list the teams people actually believe in, and make sure the options cannot overlap.

**3. The closing time.** Answers close before the outcome starts to show. For a season call, close them before the first game tips off, not after a hot first week.

**4. What counts, including the source.** This is the line that ends arguments in April. "Best record means the final regular-season standings on NBA.com. A tie counts as no." Name the source now, while nobody knows who it helps.

## A worked example

Here is the call as you would write it before Tuesday's game.

- **Question:** Will the Knicks finish with the best record in the Eastern Conference?
- **Choices:** Yes, No.
- **Answers close:** Tuesday, October 20, 7:00 PM ET, before tip-off.
- **What counts:** The final regular-season standings on NBA.com. A tie for first counts as No.

Everyone answers once. Once you submit, your answer is locked, and so are the terms. In April, the group's manager checks the standings, records the result, and the group sees who called it. Nobody has to dig through the chat, and nobody can quietly upgrade an old take.

The same four lines work for anything long. Will the show's finale reveal the killer? Will the new place on our block still be open in March? Will the new apartment be ready by the move-in date? If the call will take weeks to settle, write the terms down on the day you make it.
