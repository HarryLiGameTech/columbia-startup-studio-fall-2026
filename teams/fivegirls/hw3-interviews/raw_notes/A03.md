## Interviewer reflections

### Interpretation and open questions

The participant independently described a specific access-information gap and a recent failure with meaningful consequences. The existing “accessible” label correctly described the flat entrance but obscured the inaccessible route to the restroom when staff assistance was unavailable.

Her workaround is effortful and distributed across several sources: Google Maps, user photos, phone calls, a personal Notes list, and known public libraries. She has also attempted to correct inaccurate information once, which is stronger evidence of engagement than a positive reaction to the concept alone.

The interview challenges a binary model of accessibility. Different components of the route need separate fields, and verification should fit the type of information: photos or stable venue details for structural features, recent status reports for elevators and temporary conditions.

The concept cannot remove a physical barrier or make staff available. Its value in this case is avoiding a failed trip and enabling a better backup choice.

**Open questions:** How should the product show routes that are technically possible but depend on staff? Who can credibly verify each feature? How should stable structural facts and fast-changing elevator status use different update rules?
