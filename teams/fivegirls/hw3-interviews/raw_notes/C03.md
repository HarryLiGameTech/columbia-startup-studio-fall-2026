## Interviewer reflections

### Interpretation and open questions

The participant raised cleanliness before any targeted cleanliness prompt and described recent behavior that supports the problem: she abandoned a restroom, took an eight-minute detour, and arrived about ten minutes late. Her standard is practical usability rather than perfection.

Her current workaround is strong but narrow: a mental list works well in familiar areas and fails when she is outside her normal routes. The concept may therefore be most useful during occasional trips rather than daily campus life.

The most important design implication is to present concrete conditions and update times, not a vague cleanliness score. Her prior behavior also reveals a contribution risk: she reported missing soap to an employee but has never posted restroom information online and does not want to write a full review.

**Open questions:** Would a four-tap report be short enough for her to contribute? How recent must a report be at different venue types and times of day? Would agreement from multiple users meaningfully increase trust?
