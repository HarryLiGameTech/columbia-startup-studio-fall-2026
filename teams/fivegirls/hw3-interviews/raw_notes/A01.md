## 3 Problem exploration

### Q3 through Q7 notes

- 6:15 / Q3 "Tell me about the last time you needed a restroom while out in the city." / PARAPHRASE: Last Saturday, meeting a friend in the West Village; needed a restroom about 20 minutes before dinner. Issue raised unprompted, before any accessibility-specific prompt.
- 6:16 / Q3 / PARAPHRASE (sequence): (1) Searched Google Maps for "public restroom"; (2) went to a café listed as "wheelchair accessible"; (3) entrance had one step, no ramp; (4) tried a second café, entered, but restroom was down a narrow hallway with a door he could not get through; (5) went to a chain pharmacy, staff said restroom was "employees only"; (6) eventually used the restroom at the restaurant once his table was ready.
- 6:17 / Q3 / QUOTE: "The app said 'wheelchair accessible.' Accessible for what? The front door? The bathroom was a closet."
- 6:18 / Q4 / Timing: PARAPHRASE: Estimated about 35 minutes from first search to using a restroom.
- 6:18 / Q4 / ENTRY RESTRICTION (separate from physical barrier): Pharmacy restroom "employees only" — policy restriction, not physical.
- 6:18 / Q4 / PHYSICAL BARRIERS: café 1 entrance step; café 2 narrow hallway and door width.
- 6:19 / Q5 "How often does something like this happen?" / QUOTE: "Honestly? Every time I leave my bubble. So maybe twice a month it gets bad like that."
- 6:20 / Q6 / OBSERVATION: Voice tightened and he paused before answering; rubbed forehead when describing the pharmacy.
- 6:20 / Q6 / QUOTE: "It's not the bathroom, it's the feeling of being a problem everyone has to deal with."
- 6:21 / Q7 / PARAPHRASE: Has cut outings short or declined plans because he was unsure about restrooms; also limits fluids before long trips.
- 6:22 / Q7 / QUOTE: "I don't drink water before I go to Manhattan. Which is probably not great."

### Additional details or a problem that did not occur

- 6:23 / PARAPHRASE: Said newer buildings and big chains are "usually fine"; the problem is mostly older small businesses and anything labeled accessible without detail.
- 6:23 / PARAPHRASE: Said single-stall, gender-neutral restrooms are often easier for him than multi-stall rooms. This partly challenges our assumption that "accessible stall" is the main need.

## Interviewer reflections

### Interpretation and open questions

- INTERPRETATION: Core pain is unreliable, low-detail accessibility information more than restroom scarcity. "Accessible" labels without specifics create wasted trips and social discomfort.
- INTERPRETATION: Trust and verification are the deciding factor; he already has crowd-labeled data and doesn't trust it.
- Open question: Would his peers (other wheelchair users) agree that photos plus measurements are enough, or do needs vary a lot by mobility device?
- Open question: Behavior change (limiting water intake) suggests real cost; worth probing frequency with others.
- Contradiction to note: Single-stall/gender-neutral may matter as much as ADA stall features.
