Interviewer reflections

Interpretation and open questions

The participant experienced a real route/access-information problem, but it was situational rather than a daily accessibility need. The strongest evidence is that she abandoned one restroom and spent another 10–15 minutes finding an alternative because she did not know the bathroom was downstairs.

Her experience suggests that a binary “accessible / not accessible” label may be less useful than specific details such as entrance level, stairs, elevator availability and restroom location.

However, she did not independently search for an accessibility-specific restroom service and has not reported access information before. Her positive reaction to the concept should therefore be treated as weaker evidence than her past behavior.

Open question: interview people who depend on particular access features more consistently to see whether inaccurate information causes larger consequences.
