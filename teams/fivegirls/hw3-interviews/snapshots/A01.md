## Interview snapshot

**Who:** "Marcus", late 30s, manual wheelchair user; paralegal commuting Astoria → Lower Manhattan by subway about 4 days a week; regularly needs restrooms in Manhattan.

**Problem confirmed?** Yes, unprompted. Listings marked "wheelchair accessible" often mean only the entrance; last Saturday he tried two cafés and a pharmacy before finding a usable restroom (~35 min). Raised at 6:15 before any accessibility-specific prompt.

**Key quote:** "The app said 'wheelchair accessible.' Accessible for what? The front door? The bathroom was a closet." (Raw notes 6:17, Q3)

**Emotional intensity:** 8/10 — voice tightened, paused and rubbed forehead (6:20); "it's the feeling of being a problem everyone has to deal with"; limits water intake before trips.

**Current solution:** Google Maps accessibility filter, calling ahead (~half the time; "works maybe 70 percent"), and a mental list of three trusted spots. Fails for unfamiliar neighborhoods; 10–15 min research before trips. Deleted one restroom app for lacking photos/measurements.

**Money spent?** About $5–6 per purchase to use café restrooms, a few times a month; one cab ride home instead of searching (amount not stated).

**Solution reaction:** "If it has photos of the actual bathroom, not the storefront, yes. That's the thing nobody has." Leaned in and asked two follow-up questions; would contribute data if under a minute; would pay "a few dollars a month if it's actually reliable."

**Biggest concern:** Verification — "Who's verifying it, though? Because if it's just people tapping 'accessible,' that's what I already have."

**Referral?** Offered to ask "a couple people from my adaptive sports group"; no introduction received yet.

**Follow-up?** Agreed to test a prototype; no date set. Interviewer to email when a prototype is ready.

**Surprise:** Single-stall/gender-neutral restrooms are often easier for him than multi-stall "accessible" rooms; detail and trust, not restroom scarcity, are the core issue.

**Signal:** Green — specific recent incident, recurring (~2×/month), concrete workarounds and small spend, strong reaction tied to verified photos; uncertainty is whether one participant's needs generalize across mobility devices.
