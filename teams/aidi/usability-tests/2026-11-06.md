# Usability Test - 2026-11-06

## 1. Testing Objectives

- Evaluate whether users can discover the waitlist entry point.
- Determine whether users can provide their background information and preferences without assistance.
- Evaluate the usability of institution search and filtering.
- Determine whether users can understand institution overview and detail pages.
- Evaluate whether the sources of institutional information are clear and discoverable.

## 2. Product Version and Recent Changes

- Product version: MVP
- Testing URL: Local
- Date tested: 11/01/2026 - 11/05/2026
- Changes made this week:
  - The test version is identical to the MVP version, with no modifications whatsoever.

## 3. Participants

We conducted usability testing with 3 real users.

| ID | User Type | Target User? | Testing Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Counselor | Yes | Online |
| P2 | Senior | No | Offline |
| P3 | Pre-recruited usability tester | Yes | Online |

## 4. Testing Tasks

1. Explore the home page and any landing pages that interest you.
2. Starting from the home page, try to join the waitlist.
3. Provide your personal background information and preferences without assistance.
4. Search for educational institutions that match your interests.
5. Apply filters to narrow the search results.
6. Select an institution and review its overview and detailed information.
7. Find where the displayed institutional information comes from and explain what you found.

## 6. User Feedback

- **P1:** Described the interface as intuitive, concise, smooth, and credible. Wanted simpler preference choices, contextual explanations, complete Chinese localization, clearer cost information, better long-page navigation, and more information about demographics, daily life, transportation, jobs, and research opportunities.
- **P2:** Found the search, filters, and institution details clear and trustworthy. Wanted clearer navigation, less absolute product language, an explanation of how fit and rankings are calculated, definitions for unfamiliar terms, a visible Save or Next action, and more lifestyle information. Would consider paying more for a highly ranked institution and wanted the product to flag schools that exceed the stated budget instead of excluding them completely.
- **P3:** Described the interface as clean, professional, and trustworthy, but found several interactions unclear.

## 7. Recurring Problems and Patterns

1. All three participants encountered unfamiliar terminology or lacked context for metrics, financial-aid concepts, admissions policies, or other specialized terms.
2. All three participants could not clearly tell how to save or complete the background and preference flow.
3. P1 and P3 noticed English content remaining in the Chinese interface.
4. P1 and P3 found it difficult to navigate quickly through the institution detail page.
5. P1 and P2 wanted more practical lifestyle, location, and financial information for comparing institutions.
6. All three participants generally described the product or its information as clear, professional, or credible, although P2 questioned the early-stage appearance and absolute tone of the home page.

## 8. Product Decisions

| Finding | Decision | Reason | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Users could not tell whether their background and preference information had been saved or what to do next. | Add a clear **Save and Continue** action, a visible saved state, and a completion message. | All three participants experienced uncertainty in this core flow. | P0 |
| Users did not understand several specialized terms, metrics, and options. | Add bilingual explanations, examples, and reference ranges for specialized terminology and data. | All three participants needed additional context to make informed choices. | P1 |
| English content remained visible in the Chinese interface. | Complete the Chinese localization. Keep an English term only when an exact translation is unsuitable, and provide a Chinese explanation with it. | Mixed-language content created confusion for users of the Chinese interface. | P1 |
| Users could not quickly navigate long institution detail pages. | Add a persistent table of contents or side navigation and a clear way to return to the search results. | Faster navigation will help users locate and compare specific information. | P1 |
| Users could find the waitlist but did not always understand what would happen after joining. | Show a clear confirmation and next steps after submission, and keep the waitlist entry accessible from the top navigation. | The entry point was discoverable, but the post-submission state was unclear. | P1 |

Priority levels:

- **P0:** Must fix immediately.
- **P1:** Important and should be addressed soon.
- **P2:** Useful improvement, but not urgent.

## 9. Changes Planned for Next Week

- Add a clear Save and Continue action, a visible saved state, and a completion message to the background and preference flow.
- Complete the Chinese localization and add bilingual explanations for the highest-impact specialized terms and metrics.
- Add a persistent table of contents or side navigation to institution detail pages, with a clear way to return to the search results.
- Add a confirmation message and clear next steps after waitlist submission, and keep the waitlist entry accessible from the top navigation.

## 10. Questions for the Next Test

- Can users complete the background and preference flow and clearly tell when their information has been saved?
- Do the bilingual explanations help users understand specialized terms, metrics, financial-aid concepts, and admissions policies?
- Can users use the Chinese interface without encountering unexplained English content?
- Can users quickly navigate to a specific section of an institution detail page and return to the search results?
- After joining the waitlist, do users understand what happened and what to expect next?
