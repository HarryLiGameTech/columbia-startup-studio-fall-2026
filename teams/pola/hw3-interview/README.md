# Pola HW3 interview materials

These scripts test the problems behind Pola's two highest dot-vote results: **Unstoppable Class-Punctuality Wearable (#6, 7 votes)** and **Random Camera-Roll Snapchat (#12, 3 votes)**. The original names came from the worst-business-ideas exercise. The interviews follow the subsequent concept expansions and lean canvases, which develop useful versions of those ideas.

| Original idea | Problem to investigate | Working concept from the team folder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #6 Unstoppable Class-Punctuality Wearable | Students struggle to judge when to leave for class, causing lateness, rushing, or unnecessary waiting. | Predictive Campus Departure Assistant: estimate travel from current location to the classroom and suggest when to leave. |
| #12 Random Camera-Roll Snapchat | Students lose contact with acquaintances because restarting a conversation can feel awkward; forgotten photos may provide a reason to reconnect. | Resurface an approved photo and suggest a contact, with the user choosing whether and to whom to send it. |

These are hypotheses, not findings. For #6, distinguish inaccurate travel estimates from classes ending late or ignoring known departure times. For #12, distinguish wanting to reconnect from simply enjoying old photos. The scripts leave room for either hypothesis to be wrong.

## How to use the scripts

Plan five interviews per problem, ten total. Recruit people with relevant recent experience, preferably friends of friends or strangers rather than close friends. Use separate interview records for each conversation. Suggested IDs for future real interviews are `departure-01` through `departure-05` and `reconnection-01` through `reconnection-05`.

Each script takes **5-10 minutes**, retaining the class's six parts in a shorter form. The slides suggest about 25 minutes in the sample opening and provide section timings (PDF pages 48-50); the assignment does not state a minimum interview duration. Our five-minute core uses one simple branch: difficulty versus no difficulty. Ask permission to continue if using up to two optional follow-ups, and stop by ten minutes. Let the participant do most of the talking, skip questions already answered, and introduce the concept only in part 5. A participant who has no problem provides useful contrary evidence.

After each actual interview, copy the snapshot template and complete it within one hour. Preserve the three tables and all 12 field names from PDF pages 67-69. The metadata above the tables is a team addition to track the interview and completion time. Use exact participant words for the key quote; label interviewer observations and interpretations separately. Write `Not asked`, `Not observed`, or `Unknown` where appropriate rather than guessing.

The slides specify green, yellow, or red but do not define a scoring formula. For consistency, use this **team working convention**, with a short evidence-based reason:

- **Green:** a specific recurring pain, meaningful effort or cost to address it, and a concrete next step or other strong behavioral evidence.
- **Yellow:** some pain, but unclear severity, adequate alternatives, unresolved fit, or only tentative commitment.
- **Red:** no meaningful pain in this segment, no need beyond polite agreement, or an existing solution that already works well.

Emotional intensity is an interviewer estimate from 1 to 10, justified by observed words or behavior; it is not automatically a participant self-rating. Keep a promised referral distinct from an introduction actually made, and follow-up willingness distinct from a scheduled meeting. A positive concept reaction does not establish demand. After real interviews, compare five snapshots per problem for repeated pain; exclude these dummy examples from that comparison.
