# Social reconnection interview script

**Idea #12:** Controlled photo resurfacing and sharing. **Target:** Five students who recently met acquaintances through classes, clubs, dorms, or events.

**Length: 5-10 minutes.** The six core sections total about five minutes. Follow only the applicable branch; use at most two optional follow-ups if they have more time. Let them talk; skip questions already answered. Do not introduce photos or the concept before section 5 unless they bring up photos themselves.

## 1 Opening

**15 seconds.**

> Hi, I'm [name], doing a Columbia class project on keeping in touch with people you meet on campus. Do you have five minutes, and is it okay if I take notes? It's helpful to hear what already works, too.

## 2 Context

**30 seconds.**

> Think of someone you recently met through a class, club, dorm, or event who isn't a close friend. How have you kept in touch?

## 3 Recent experience

**2 minutes.**

> What's the hardest part, if anything, about keeping in touch with people like that?

- **If they describe a difficulty:** “Tell me about the last time you wanted to reach out. What did you do, and what happened?” Then: “Why did that matter to you, and how often has it happened in the past month?”
- **If it works fine:** “What makes it easy? Tell me about your last interaction.” Accept that letting some connections fade may be intentional and move on.

Do not supply “awkwardness” as the answer. Participants can describe messages without displaying them or naming contacts.

## 4 Current solution

**45 seconds.**

> What have you tried to keep in touch, and how well does it work? Have you spent money specifically on this? How much?

Photo-storage spending alone does not establish spending on reconnection.

## 5 Concept reaction

**1 minute.**

> We're exploring a tool that resurfaces a photo from a collection you approve and suggests an acquaintance to send it to. You choose the recipient and approve every send. What's your first reaction? What's your biggest concern?

Capture their first words. Enjoying old photos is different from needing help reconnecting; record those separately.

## 6 Close

**30 seconds.**

> Could you introduce us to someone with a similar experience? May we contact you for a brief follow-up?

Thank them. Record an introduction or scheduled time only if it actually happens.

## Optional follow-ups

Use at most two, only if they agree to continue beyond five minutes. Stop by ten minutes.

- **They wanted contact but did not reach out:** “What stopped you that time?”
- **Photos seem relevant:** “When did you last revisit an old photo? What did you do next?” Distinguish sharing with an acquaintance from private nostalgia.
- **Concept concern:** “Have you tried something similar? What happened?” For a photo-access or recipient concern, ask for their last experience with that issue.
