# Session 1

**Date:** Nov 6th, 2026

## Participant Background

A railway enthusiast based in Beijing, with moderate-to-good spatial awareness and strong computer literacy. Familiar with complex transportation environments, but less experienced with intricate indoor layouts, especially less obvious connections and passages

## Task-by-task observations

### Task 1: Join the waitlist

**We said:** "This is AstraPath's website. Find out what it does, then join the waitlist."

- **What they did:** Initially skimmed the landing page to understand the product's purpose. Looked for concrete examples of indoor navigation rather than relying exclusively on the headline. Located and used the waitlist registration button without technical difficulty. 
- **What they said out loud:** "So it's basically navigation for inside buildings? Like a map that tells you which corridors and elevators to take? That'll be really useful"
- **Completed?** Yes

### Task 2: Find the room

**We said:** "You've just walked into the Yintai Center, and suppose your meeting will be held in room 3007 in 10 minutes. Find how to get there. You can try the AstraPath web app thru the URL we've just provided to you"

- **Entrance and room used:** South subway linking passage
- **What they did:** Initially tried to find the room by following the building's signs without using the navigation app. However, he took a wrong turn and ended up in the residential apartment building's lobby. After realizing how complicated the building's layout was, he decided to open AstraPath and successfully generated a route to room 3007.
- **What they said out loud:** "Okay, I definitely need the app for this. Otherwise I'm gonna be late, especially in this kinda maze-like place"
- **Completed?** Partially (successfully found a route in the app, but failed to physically reach there in time)

### Task 3: Before you walk (aha moment)

**We said:** "Now, before you follow the route, take a look at the directions and tell me how you'd get to the room."

- **What they did:** Reviewed the full route and mentally walked through the instructions, paying close attention to corridor connections, elevator choices, and floor transitions.
- **What they said out loud:** "Okay, so I can get the whole route display even before I start walking in here, including which elevator to take. That's actually useful in a building I've never been to."
- **Completed?** Yes

### Task 4: A route without stairs

**We said:** "Imagine you're on crutches today. Get a route to the same room that doesn't use stairs."

- **What they did:** Initially overlooked the "Step-free" checkbox in the app. After noticing it, he enabled the option and observed that the first few steps of the route had changed. He compared the new directions with the original route and realized that the stairs between the subway entrance, LG, and L levels had been replaced with elevator rides.
- **What they said out loud:** "Oh, so now I just take the elevators from the subway up to LG and then L, instead of using the escalators. A bit of a detour, maybe an extra half a minute or so."
- **Completed?** Partially (successfully generated and understood the step-free route, but did not physically reach the destination within 10 minutes).

## Post-session answers

**1. In your own words, what does this product do?** 

"It helps people navigate inside complicated buildings, especially when you need to move between different floors or find the right elevator. Kind of like Google Maps, but for the inside of buildings."

**2. What was the first moment you understood what this is for?**  

"Probably when I saw how it could give me directions to a specific room, not just the building or the floor. Knowing the elevator and the path beforehand is the useful part."

**3. What was the most confusing part?**

"Figuring out which starting node to select. I wasn't entirely sure which one matched my actual location."

**4. Is there anything you expected to find that wasn't there?**  

"McDonald's on LG floor. I couldn't find it on the map. Maybe the map's annotation data is a bit outdated."

**5. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**  

"Yes, especially in big complexes, stations, or other buildings I'm not familiar with. I'm usually okay with finding my way around, but knowing the route in advance would save time. It would depend on how many buildings are supported (in the future), though."

**6. If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing, what would it be?**  

"I'd love it if the app could just figure out exactly where I am, so I wouldn't have to pick a starting point manually. Though I guess that's pretty expensive to implement technically."

**7. When was the last time you had trouble finding a room inside a building? What did you do?**  

"Probably when I was traveling through Wuhan Railway Station. Its layout is pretty unusual compared to most other railway stations in China. I spent way longer than expected trying to find the right platform entrance and almost missed my train."

## Quick insight

The biggest thing this session showed us was that even users with relatively good spatial awareness can struggle in complex indoor environments, and AstraPath can help by making multi-floor routes understandable before navigation begins. However, manually identifying the correct starting node remains a major usability barrier. Improving location awareness, making navigation settings more discoverable, and keeping map annotations up to date could significantly improve the user experience.
