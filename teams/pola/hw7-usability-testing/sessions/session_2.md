**Stranger or someone we know?** Someone we just met

**Recording consent?** No

## Participant Background

A middle-aged startup lawyer with average spatial awareness and strong reading comprehension skills. Generally confident navigating familiar environments and comfortable following detailed written instructions. However, has slight difficulty identifying distant text-based visual landmarks (e.g. smaller shop logos), particularly in unfamiliar indoor spaces.

## Task-by-task observations

### Task 1: Join the waitlist

**We said:** "This is AstraPath's website. Find out what it does, then join the waitlist."

- **What they did:** Read through the landing page carefully, paying attention to the product description and its practical applications. Understood the general purpose of the service and proceeded to the waitlist registration without significant difficulty
- **What they said out loud:** "So this is meant to help people find their way around large buildings, right? That sounds pretty practical."
- **Completed?** Yes

### Task 2: Find the room

**We said:** "You've just entered Hudson Yards Mall, and imagine you need to grab a quick lunch at Chipotle. Find your way there using AstraPath."

- **Entrance and room used:** The gate that is closest to 50 Hudson Yards; Chipotle on 4th floor
- **What they did:** Opened the app, selected the starting location and destination, and carefully reviewed the generated directions. Had no difficulty understanding the written instructions, but expressed some uncertainty about identifying the exact turning points along longer corridors
- **What they said out loud:** "The instructions are pretty clear. I'm just not sure how far I'm supposed to go before making the next turn. I can't really see THAT far ahead, especially when the signs and logos are so small."
- **Completed?** Yes (though almost made 1 wrong turn)

### Task 3: Before you walk (aha moment)

**We said:** "Now, before you follow the route, take a look at the directions and tell me how you'd get to the room."

- **What they did:** Reviewed the complete route and accurately explained the sequence of corridors, elevators, and floor transitions. Demonstrated strong comprehension of the instructions, but pointed out that understanding a route beforehand does not necessarily mean being able to recognize every turning point while walking.
- **What they said out loud:** "Okay, I can see exactly how I'm supposed to get there. That's nice. But I'd still be a little worried about missing a turn, especially if I'm told to just keep going straight for quite a while."
- **Completed?** Yes

### Task 4: A route without stairs

**We said:** "Imagine you're on crutches today. Get a route to the same room that doesn't use stairs or escalators."

- **What they did:** Looked for an accessibility-related option, selected the "Step-free" checkbox without hesitation, then reviewed the updated route. Compared the revised directions with the original ones to identify which sections had changed.
- **What they said out loud:** "Okay, so it changes the route to only use elevators when traveling between floors. That makes sense, quite friendly for the disabled and the ones with large suitcases"
- **Completed?** Yes

## Post-session answers

**1. In your own words, what does this product do?** 

"It's a navigation tool for large buildings. It helps you find a specific place like shops or facilities, and tells you exactly how to get there, including which elevators and corridors to use"

**2. What was the first moment you understood what this is for?**  

"When I saw that it could give me detailed directions to a particular room inside a building. That's quite useful when you're visiting an unfamiliar office. Because, you know, taking the wrong elevator can be quite awkward"

**3. What was the most confusing part?**

"Some of the longer walking instructions. I understand what they mean, but if I can't see where I'm supposed to turn, I start wondering whether I've already missed it"

**4. Is there anything you expected to find that wasn't there?**  

"I didn't find any, everything reflected the physical environment quite correctly"

**5. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**  

"Yes, especially when visiting unfamiliar offices or large commercial buildings. It's convenient to know where you're going beforehand. I'd just feel more comfortable if I could tell more easily whether I'm still on the right track."

**6. If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing, what would it be?**  

"I'd like a few more reference points along the way, something like 'Go from A to B via B and C'. Sometimes just telling me to keep going straight isn't enough. It'd be nice to have something that reassures me I'm still heading in the right direction."

**7. When was the last time you had trouble finding a specific place inside a building? What did you do?** 

"When I was on a business trip to the World Trade Center, I saw an elevator sign saying 'Floors 64–90,' so I assumed I could go straight up. Turns out I had to transfer at the sky lobby on the 64th floor, which wasn't clearly explained. I spent some extra minutes figuring out which elevator to take next"

## Quick insight

The biggest thing this session could show us was that understanding navigation instructions does not necessarily translate into confidence while following them. Even users with strong reading comprehension may feel uncertain when long route segments lack recognizable intermediate landmarks. More frequent and context-aware guidance could help reduce this uncertainty, particularly for users who have difficulty recognizing distant turning points.
