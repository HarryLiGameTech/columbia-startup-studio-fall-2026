## Participant Background

A first-year undergraduate recruited on campus, with average spatial awareness and high comfort using a phone browser. Uses Google Maps confidently outdoors and usually trusts lobby signs once inside a building. Has walked past Northwest Corner but had never been in it, and tends to treat a room number like 502 as a label rather than a floor.

## Task-by-task observations

### Task 1: Join the waitlist

**We said:** "This is AstraPath's website. Find out what it does, then join the waitlist."

- **What they did:** Read the headline and the line under it, then scrolled until the page said maps stop at the front door. Found the waitlist near the bottom, left "I'm finding my way" selected, entered an email, and submitted. The page switched to "You're on the list."
- **What they said out loud:** "So it's directions after you're already at the building. Which door, which elevator. Not another campus map. The form was obvious."
- **Completed?** Yes

### Task 2: Find the room

**We said:** "Stay on this website. You've just walked in the West Gate of Northwest Corner, and your class is in room 502. Find how to get there."

- **Entrance and room used:** West Gate, floor 1; room 502
- **What they did:** Looked for a map on the page. Opened the menu and clicked "Columbia demo," which left the site. We asked them to come back. They used "Try a route" and typed "Northwest Corner 502." The card that came back was the water fountain: stairs from 1 to 2, escalator from 2 to 4, library stairs to 5. They cleared the box and typed "502" alone. That card was room 502: elevator to 5, turn right, third door on the right.
- **What they said out loud:** "I typed the building and it sent me to a fountain. The classroom only showed up when I typed the number by itself."
- **Completed?** Yes (after a wrong preview and one trip off the page)

### Task 3: Before you walk (aha moment)

**We said:** "Without walking anywhere yet, tell me how you would get to the room."

- **What they did:** Read the room 502 card from the top and said it back without leaving the chair. They named the elevator, the floors that car actually stops at, and the door count after the turn. They also noticed the Columbia plate is a different trip from the same West Gate, stairs and an escalator to a fountain, not to 502.
- **What they said out loud:** "I don't have to walk it. This car goes to 1, 3, and 5, so I get off on 5 and 502 is the third door on the right. I was going to follow the stairs on the other card. That one isn't even my room."
- **Completed?** Yes

### Task 4: A route without stairs

**We said:** "Imagine you're on crutches today. Get a route to the same room that doesn't use stairs."

- **What they did:** Looked for a switch on the route card and did not find one. The 502 card was already the elevator route. They opened the Step-free tab, which describes the same car, then clicked the "Step-free to 502" example and got that same card again. They tried searching "stairs to 502" and still got the elevator. They could not pull up a stair route to room 502 to compare.
- **What they said out loud:** "It's already the elevator. I can't turn stairs off, because this page doesn't have a stair way to 502. Step-free is just another example, not a button on the route."
- **Completed?** Yes (the no-stairs route was the only route the page would give for 502)

## Post-session answers

**1. In your own words, what does this product do?**

"It tells you how to get to a room inside the building. Which elevator, which floors that elevator actually stops on, and which door. The page I used only does that for a few examples."

**2. What was the first moment you understood what this is for?**

"When the 502 card said the elevator serves 1, 3, and 5, and then the third door on the right. Before that I thought I was just reading a marketing site."

**3. What was the most confusing part?**

"The search. I put in Northwest Corner and the room, and it gave me a water fountain. I also wasn't sure if I was supposed to leave for the Columbia demo."

**4. Is there anything you expected to find that wasn't there?**

"A real choice for the same room. Stairs or no stairs. And a search that understands the building and the room together. I expected a map, not four example buttons."

**5. Would you use this again? Why or why not?**

"The route card, yes, the first time I had class in a building like that. This page, only if my room was one of the examples. I wouldn't install anything. I'd want it in the browser."

**6. If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing, what would it be?**

"Let me type the building and the room and get that room. And put 'no stairs' on the route I'm already looking at, instead of making it a separate example."

**7. When was the last time you had trouble finding a room inside a building? What did you do?**

"During orientation I had to find a room in Lerner. The signs sent me up a floor, then the room was back down a different stair. I asked someone at the desk. I never figured out the numbering."

## Quick insight

The biggest thing this session showed us was that the website can make a route clear before anyone walks, and that was the moment P3 understood AstraPath: the elevator named, the floors it reaches, the door counted. Everything before that card was fragile. The search treats a building name and a room number as different examples, so "Northwest Corner 502" came back as the fountain, and step-free is another sample trip rather than a choice on the route in front of you. On a site with no separate app, the route search has to answer the trip the person actually asks for.
