# Interview Notes: Anonymous Student (Campus Food Waste)

## What I learned

- **Who:** Anonymous Barnard senior who lives on campus part-time, has a low meal plan, and participates in several campus jobs and organizations.
- **Problem confirmed?:** Yes. They have seen leftover food at many events and independently described difficulty deciding what to eat on campus. Most events they attend have some food remaining.
- **Key quote:** "Most of the times, the events that I've attended, it's always like, yeah, we do have some food left over."
- **Emotional intensity:** 7/10.

## Solutions and reaction

- **Current solution:** Learns about free food mainly through group chats, word of mouth, and sometimes social media. Also attends events partly because food will be available.
- **Money spent?:** Yes, indirectly. A low meal plan sometimes leads them to buy food off campus. They also bring food from home and cook to conserve meal points.
- **Solution reaction:** Very positive. Independently suggested a platform where clubs could post available food live. They said students would "definitely be interested" in notifications, especially when food could be picked up without attending the event.
- **Biggest concern:** Stale information. They need food type, quantity, location, and remaining availability. They prefer live updates because a 30-minute pickup window does not guarantee food remains on arrival.

## Commitments and signal

- **Referral?:** No referral requested or offered.
- **Follow-up?:** No explicit follow-up agreement.
- **Surprise:** Type, quantity, location, and timing matter together. A generic free-food notification is not sufficient. They preferred live quantity updates rather than a pickup window alone.
- **Signal:** Green.
