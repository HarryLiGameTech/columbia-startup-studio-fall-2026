# Synthesis comparison

Team Aditya. Five interviews about how students find off-campus housing and subleases in NYC: H1 Ruby, H2 Emma, H3 Tina, H4 Marcus, H5 Jasmine. Sources: [raw notes](../hw3-interviews/02-raw-interview-notes.md) and [snapshots](../hw3-interviews/03-interview-snapshots.md). The group-chat interviews (G1 to G5) belong to the idea we dropped and are left out here.

## Part A: Our synthesis

### Key patterns

**1. Finding housing is hard, and people just want it done.** Nobody described the search as something they enjoyed. It was a task to finish. Emma toured about 30 places in one week, about 10 in a single day, and called it "absolutely awful" (9/10 intensity). Marcus needed a room before the semester started and felt like he was wasting time (8/10). The goal is a signed lease, not a good browsing session.

**2. The search is scattered across too many places.** Emma used Zillow, StreetEasy, Realtor, Marketplace, Ohana, Instagram and word of mouth. Marcus used Facebook Marketplace, school housing groups and group chats. Jasmine went through Facebook groups, rental websites and Instagram. That's 3 of 5 people juggling 3 to 7 sources at once, plus messaging every lister separately. This is where a lot of the wasted time comes from.

**3. You don't get what you imagined.** Listings don't match reality. Emma's filters left out important details, photos were misleading (some looked AI-generated), and she had to message landlords to confirm basics like whether pets were allowed. Marcus wasn't sure half the people he messaged were even real. Seeing a place you liked online and then finding out it isn't what you pictured, or isn't real at all, is a big part of the frustration.

**4. Trust is the gate before anyone sends money.** 4 of 5 raised it: Ruby, Emma, Marcus and Jasmine. For Marcus and Jasmine it's about scams and fake people. For Emma it's about inaccurate listings. For Ruby it's fear of being scammed at all.

**5. A personal connection skips the whole problem.** Tina sublet from her cousin and never searched. Jasmine's problem ended once she found a sublease through mutual friends. When people have a trusted lead, they don't need a platform.

### Top 5 quotes (exact words)

1. "We visited, like, 30 places, and we messaged probably way more than that." (Emma, H2)
2. "It was absolutely awful." (Emma, H2)
3. "I messaged probably like twenty people and half of them never answered. The other half I wasn't even sure were real." (Marcus, H4)
4. "I'd rather rent from somebody who knows somebody I know than just send money to a random person online." (Jasmine, H5)
5. "I think I'd rather be, like, not scammed than to know if that community is, like, an ideal place for me." (Ruby, H1)

More exact quotes from our submitted interviews:

- "No. I didn't look at a single other place." (Tina, H3, the negative case: a cousin's lease meant no search at all)
- "I might actually just get scammed." (Ruby, H1, who also called herself very trusting)
- "maybe would be a really good tool" (Ruby, H1, reacting to a verified student platform)

Heard in our interviews but not written in our notes, so these come from Lookman's memory and the speaker wasn't recorded:

- "There was so many sites I forgot which one I used." (backs pattern 2: the search is scattered across too many places)
- "My brother found it for me." (backs pattern 5: a family connection skips the search, like Tina's cousin)

### Contradictions, and what they might mean

**People like browsing and tailoring, but hate the constant looking.** In the room, people seemed fine with picking what they want: location, size, pets, laundry, a doorman. Emma even liked touring an apartment alone and asked for a map of just her favorite listings. But the same people called the overall process awful. Our read is that deciding what you want is the fun part. The draining part is the repetition: checking site after site, messaging people who don't reply, scheduling tours, and being let down when a place isn't what you imagined. This is our impression from the interviews; nobody said it in these words, and our notes don't capture it directly.

**More listings vs. fewer, trusted ones.** Emma wanted listings from every site in one place, and Marcus said an app is only useful with enough listings. Jasmine cared less about volume and more about knowing the person was real. This could be two different users: someone hunting a long-term apartment who needs options to compare, and someone looking for a short sublease who needs to trust one person.

**A platform vs. word of mouth.** Emma and Marcus wanted a better tool. Tina and, to a degree, Ruby prefer people they know. The dividing line looks like whether you already have a trusted contact in NYC.

**Willingness to pay.** Marcus would consider paying a small amount for verified listings. Emma wouldn't pay $10 in her situation, and Jasmine wouldn't pay just to browse. People may pay for safety but not for search.

### Surprises: what changed our thinking

- **Scheduling tours was as painful as finding listings.** We assumed the pain was discovery. For Emma, the back-and-forth with agents was a big part of it.
- **Trust beat volume.** For Marcus the problem wasn't finding listings but knowing which were real. Jasmine said the same.
- **Ruby fears scams but calls herself very trusting.** She said "I might actually just get scammed." The people most worried about scams may also be the most exposed to them.
- **Even big platforms let people down.** Emma used all the major sites and still found the filters and photos unreliable.

### Our overall read

Students looking for housing are frustrated because the search is long, scattered and hard to trust, and they just want to get it done. The data points toward a better, unified search platform: one place that pulls listings from the sites people already use, removes duplicates, and shows what's real (accurate details, verified people, school or mutual connections) so there are fewer let-downs. The caveats: this is five interviews, one person (Tina) had no problem at all, and nobody has shown they would pay. Trust and the number of listings are the two things that would make or break it.

## Data caveats (read first)

- **The raw notes for H2–H5 are labeled "paraphrased."** The H1 raw notes are four short fragments ("Cares about germs," "Wants a brutal honest," and two others). ## 1. Patterns

### Theme A: Fear of scams, and not knowing whether the person or listing is real
- **Appears in:** H1 (Ruby), H4 (Marcus), H5 (Jasmine)
- **Strength:** Strong.
  - **Marcus (8/10):** said the biggest problem was not knowing which listings were trustworthy. Facebook Marketplace listings "looked suspicious."
  - **Jasmine (6/10):** "did not really trust sending money to someone she had never met." Trust was her biggest concern.
  - **Ruby (6/10):** scams were part of her biggest concern, along with trust and authenticity. She put avoiding scams above knowing whether a community suits her.
- **What it is:** the fear that the person posting is fake, or that money sent to a stranger will be lost.
- **Not included here:** Emma (H2). Her trust problem was about inaccurate listing details, not fraud. See Theme B.

### Theme B: Listing information that is inaccurate or incomplete
- **Appears in:** H2 (Emma) only
- **Strength:** Strong for her (9/10 overall for the search).
  - Filters were unreliable and left out important details.
  - Photos could be misleading, possibly AI-generated.
  - She contacted landlords directly to confirm details the listings left out.
  - Because she has a pet, she had to confirm each place was pet-friendly before she could even tour it.
- **Why it is separate from Theme A:** Emma's problem was data quality on established platforms (Zillow, StreetEasy, Realtor). It was not about people being fraudulent.

### Theme C: Searching takes a huge amount of time and outreach
This theme has two different mechanisms, so I list them separately.
- **C1: Arranging tours with agents. H2 (Emma) only.**
  - She toured about 30 places in one week, about 10 in one day.
  - Scheduling viewings with agents was "annoying" and took a lot of back-and-forth.
  - Strength: very strong (9/10).
- **C2: People who never reply. H4 (Marcus) only.**
  - He messaged about twenty people and half never answered.
  - He had to message many people in the school housing group before he found a room.
  - He was under deadline pressure to find housing before the semester started.
  - Strength: strong (8/10).

### Theme D: Personal networks and word of mouth as the trusted route
- **Appears in:** H3 (Tina), H5 (Jasmine), H1 (Ruby), H4 (Marcus, partly), H2 (Emma, mentioned only)
- **Strength:** varies by person.
  - **Tina:** this was her whole method. She sublet from her cousin and did not look at any other place. Her process was "much easier."
  - **Jasmine:** this is how she finally solved it. She found the place through a friend of a friend's Instagram post. Mutual friends made her feel "much more comfortable."
  - **Ruby:** her family has found housing mainly through friends and familiar cultural communities. She worries a platform would lose that.
  - **Marcus:** found his room through a school housing group and also uses friends' recommendations. That is an affiliation network, but the notes do not say he trusted it more.
  - **Emma:** listed word of mouth, friends and Instagram as common channels among others. The notes do not show she relied on them.

### Theme E: Interest in verification, mutual connections or school affiliation
- **Appears in:** H4 (Marcus), H5 (Jasmine), H1 (Ruby, more weakly)
- **Strength:** moderate, and prompted by the concept.
  - **Marcus:** "especially liked" seeing someone's school or mutual connections.
  - **Jasmine:** liked seeing mutual connections, school affiliation, or some kind of verification.
  - **Ruby:** said it "maybe would be a really good tool" and liked that it could reduce scams.
- **Caveat:** these are reactions to the concept, not needs people raised on their own.

### Theme F: Doubts about adopting a new platform (popularity, number of listings, need to switch)
Three different reasons, kept separate:
- **H2 (Emma):** prefers familiar platforms like StreetEasy. She would switch only if the new app became trusted and popular in the community.
- **H4 (Marcus):** his biggest concern was whether enough people would use it. It would only be useful with a good number of listings.
- **H3 (Tina):** has no reason to switch away from family and word of mouth.
- **Strength:** moderate. For Emma and Marcus it was their stated biggest concern.

### Theme G: Low willingness to pay
- **Appears in:** H2, H4, H5. Not mentioned for H1 or H3.
  - **Emma:** would not pay an agent. She might pay a small fee if moving from far away, but would not pay $10 in her situation.
  - **Marcus:** would "consider paying a small amount" for scam avoidance or verified listings.
  - **Jasmine:** would "probably not pay just to browse listings."
- **Strength:** low. Nobody spent money on search tools.

### Theme H: Cleanliness, contamination, and fit with a community or culture
- **Appears in:** H1 (Ruby) only
- **Strength:** clear for her. The raw notes say "Cares about germs." She worries a platform cannot show a neighborhood's community, history, cleanliness or cultural fit.
- No other interviewee raised this.

## 3. Contradictions

**1. Number of listings vs. trust in the person**
- **The disagreement:**
  - Marcus said the app is only useful with enough listings.
  - Emma wanted listings gathered from many sites with duplicates removed.
  - Jasmine "cared less about having a huge number of listings and more about knowing that the person behind the listing was trustworthy."
- **Possible explanations:**
  - Different needs. Marcus wanted a semester-long room under deadline pressure. Emma wanted a long-term lease with pet constraints and needed many options. Jasmine needed a short summer sublease.
  - Different starting points. Jasmine had a network that eventually produced one good option. Marcus and Emma had to search the open market.

**2. Whether a platform is needed at all**
- **The disagreement:**
  - Tina would rather use word of mouth and has no reason to adopt an app.
  - Emma and Marcus wanted a better platform.
- **Possible explanation:** whether someone already has a trusted contact with housing in NYC. Tina had a cousin with a lease. The others did not, or did not at first.

**3. Willingness to pay**
- **The disagreement:**
  - Marcus would consider paying a small amount.
  - Jasmine probably would not pay to browse.
  - Emma would not pay $10 in her situation, but might pay a small fee if moving from far away.
- **Possible explanations:**
  - They may be talking about paying for different things. Marcus was asked about verification and scam avoidance. Jasmine's "no" was about browsing.
  - Situation matters. Distance and urgency seem to affect willingness, as Emma's own caveat suggests.

**4. Within one person (Ruby): a platform as protection vs. a platform as a loss**
- **The tension:** Ruby likes that a platform could reduce scams. She also worries it would lose the personal and cultural context of referrals through her community.
- **Possible explanation:** two separate needs, safety and belonging, that may pull different ways. This is one person's ambivalence, not a disagreement between people.

**5. Which channels people trust**
- **The difference:**
  - Emma prefers familiar platforms like StreetEasy.
  - Marcus and Jasmine distrusted Facebook Marketplace, Facebook groups and rental websites.
- **Possible explanation:** they were dealing with different kinds of market.
  - Emma dealt with landlord and agent listings, where the risk is inaccuracy.
  - Marcus and Jasmine dealt with person-to-person rooms and subleases, where the risk is fraud.
- **Caveat:** the notes do not say whether Emma ever used Facebook Marketplace for subleases specifically. She lists "Marketplace" among her tools.

## 4. Surprises

- **Ruby may be very exposed to scams even though she fears them.** She called herself very trusting and said, "I might actually just get scammed."
- **Contamination and germs (Ruby)** are a concern unlike anyone else's. The notes do not say how germs relate to housing for her: shared or sublet spaces, a previous tenant, or something else.
- **Photos may be AI-generated (Emma).** This is a newer form of listing inaccuracy.
- **For Marcus, finding listings was not the main problem.** The main problem was knowing which listings and people were trustworthy.
- **Emma specifically asked for a map showing only her favorite listings.** It is a concrete feature request that does not belong to any wider theme.
- **"Wants a brutal honest" (H1 raw notes).** The fragment is incomplete. It might mean she wants brutally honest reviews or information about places. The notes do not support a firm reading.
- **H1's second raw-note line is garbled:** "could this tell me about the community and getting usually referrals, not much of a inhuman." The snapshot reads it as a worry about losing human, community-based referrals. That is plausible, but the raw text is unclear.

## 5. Segments

These are tentative, with only five interviews. The main dividing line seems to be **whether the student has access to a trusted personal network**. A second line is **what kind of housing they need** (long-term lease vs. room or sublease vs. short-term).

**Segment 1: Housed through their network, little or no pain (H3 Tina)**
- Found housing through family. Did no search. Intensity 1/10.
- Sees little reason to use a new platform.

**Segment 2: Open-market lease hunter, pain from logistics and accuracy (H2 Emma)**
- Wants a long-term apartment, with hard requirements (pet, laundry, doorman).
- Uses mainstream platforms.
- Pain comes from volume, scheduling tours, unreliable filters and misleading photos. Intensity 9/10.
- Trusts established platforms and would switch only to a popular, trusted one.
- Fraud was not her stated concern.

**Segment 3: Person-to-person room or sublease seeker, pain from trust (H4 Marcus, H5 Jasmine)**
- Searches Facebook Marketplace, Facebook groups and school groups.
- Pain comes from fake-looking listings, no replies, and risk with money.
- Drawn to verification, mutual connections and school affiliation.
- The two differ:
  - Marcus wanted more listings and was under semester deadline pressure (8/10).
  - Jasmine wanted trust over volume and was looking for a short summer stay (6/10).
- Jasmine's network eventually solved her problem, so she partly overlaps with Segment 1.

**Segment 4: Inexperienced and anxious about a future search (H1 Ruby)**
- Lives in campus housing now, with little direct subleasing experience.
- Low-income, first-generation student.
- Worried in advance about scams, cleanliness and cultural fit.
- Values community-based referrals.
- Her concerns are mostly expectations of a future search, not lived experience. Treat them as weaker evidence about the actual search.

## Part C: The comparison

The AI was stricter than we were about keeping ideas apart, and that taught us something. We had lumped "trust" into one 4-of-5 pattern. The AI split it into two different problems: fraud (Marcus, Jasmine, Ruby: is this person real?) and listing accuracy (Emma: is this information right?). It also split "the search takes forever" into tour scheduling (Emma) and people not replying (Marcus). Those splits matter for the product, because aggregating listings fixes neither fraud nor unanswered messages. The AI's segments were also sharper than ours: an open-market lease hunter like Emma and a person-to-person sublet seeker like Marcus or Jasmine want different things, which explains the "more listings vs. trusted listings" contradiction better than we did. It also caught details we skipped: Ruby's concern about germs and cleanliness, her garbled raw-note lines, and that "solution reactions" were prompted by our pitch and so are weaker evidence. On the other side, the AI missed what we felt in the room. It never named the "just get it done" mindset, and it treated pulling listings together as one of Emma's feature requests rather than the main pattern. We saw 3 of 5 people juggling several sites and think the scattered search is central. It also had nothing on our "people like tailoring but hate the constant looking" contradiction. That is fair, because the notes don't record it. It's our impression, and we should test it in the next round of interviews instead of treating it as a finding.

We checked every quote. All five of the AI's quotes match our snapshots word for word, and they're the same ones we picked, except that it chose Tina where we chose Emma's "absolutely awful." But in its patterns section, the AI put quotation marks around phrases from our paraphrased notes as if people had said them. Examples are Jasmine "did not really trust sending money to someone she had never met" and Marcus's listings "looked suspicious." Those are our notes' words, not theirs. The AI did not merge different people's statements into a single quote. Its biggest caveat holds for us too: none of the exact quotes appear in our raw notes, only in the snapshots, so next time we should record or transcribe interviews. The same gap explains part of what the AI missed: lines we remember hearing, like "There was so many sites I forgot which one I used," never made it into our notes, so the AI had no way to see how scattered the search felt.
